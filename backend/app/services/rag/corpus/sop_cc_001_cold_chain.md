## §1 Temperature Bands
Temperature-controlled shipments are assigned one of three bands. Pharmaceutical and vaccine cargo travels in the 2 to 8 °C band. Chilled food travels between 0 and 5 °C. Frozen cargo must be kept at or below -18 °C. The temperature band is stored on the shipment and only refrigerated vehicles (reefers) rated for that band may be assigned.

## §2 Pre-Cooling and Loading
The reefer unit must be pre-cooled to the target band before loading begins, and the cargo must already be at the correct temperature when it is handed over. Loading should take place at a temperature-controlled dock, and door-open time should be kept as short as possible. Pallets must not block the return-air path of the refrigeration unit.

## §3 Monitoring and Data Logging
The cargo temperature is logged at least every 15 minutes by a calibrated data logger, and readings are streamed to the telematics platform. The data logger record forms part of the delivery proof and is retained for at least 3 years for audit.

## §4 Temperature Excursions
A temperature excursion is any reading outside the assigned band. An excursion lasting more than 30 minutes, or any reading more than 2 °C outside the band, must be reported to dispatch immediately. The affected cargo is quarantined on delivery and the Quality team decides whether it can be released, returned or destroyed. Excursions are recorded in the audit log.

## §5 Route Planning for Cold Chain
Cold chain stops are prioritised early in the route sequence to minimise time in transit, and the planned door-open duration at each stop counts toward the excursion risk. Multi-drop cold chain routes should be limited to 12 stops per vehicle. If a reefer unit fails in transit, the trip is marked ROUTE_BLOCKED and the copilot proposes a transfer of temperature-sensitive cargo to the nearest available reefer.
