## §1 Fit-for-Duty Declaration
Before every shift the driver confirms in the mobile app that they are fit for duty: rested, not under the influence of alcohol or impairing medication, and not unwell. A driver who cannot make the declaration must not start the trip, and dispatch must re-assign the route. Zero tolerance applies to alcohol: any positive breath test results in immediate removal from duty.

## §2 Recognising Fatigue on the Road
Warning signs of fatigue include frequent yawning, heavy eyelids, drifting out of lane, missing road signs and difficulty remembering the last few kilometres. A driver experiencing any of these signs must stop at the next safe location and rest for at least 20 minutes before continuing, even if no legal break is yet due. The rest stop is reported to dispatch as a DELAYED event so downstream ETAs can be recalculated.

## §3 Night Driving Restrictions
Driving between 00:00 and 05:00 carries the highest fatigue risk. Routes should not be planned with more than 2 consecutive hours of driving inside this window. Where night driving is unavoidable, the continuous driving limit before a break is reduced from 4.5 hours to 3 hours, and long-haul night trips must be double-crewed.

## §4 Shift Scheduling
Dispatchers must not schedule a driver for a shift starting less than 11 hours after the end of their previous shift. Split shifts and early starts must be rotated fairly, and a driver may not be scheduled for more than 6 consecutive working days without a weekly rest period of at least 24 hours.
