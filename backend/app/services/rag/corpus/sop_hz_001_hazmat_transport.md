## §1 Scope and Applicability
This policy applies to every shipment flagged as hazardous material (hazmat), also called dangerous goods, including flammable liquids, compressed gases, corrosives, oxidizers, toxic substances and lithium batteries. A shipment is treated as hazmat when its UN number falls in any of the nine UN hazard classes. The dispatcher must confirm the hazard class before a hazmat shipment is assigned to a route.

## §2 Driver Qualification and Training
Only drivers holding a valid commercial licence with a hazardous goods endorsement may drive a vehicle carrying hazmat. In India, Rule 9 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules requires the driver of a dangerous goods carriage to have completed a recognised hazardous goods training course and to be able to read and write. For US operations a CDL with the H (hazmat) endorsement is mandatory; under ADR the driver must hold a valid ADR training certificate. Driver training certificates must be renewed before expiry, and an expired certificate makes the driver ineligible for hazmat assignment.

## §3 Placarding, Labelling and Weight Thresholds
Every package must carry the correct hazard class label, and the vehicle must display placards or orange-coloured plates as required. Under US DOT rules (49 CFR 172.504), Table 1 materials such as explosives and poison-inhalation hazards require placards in any quantity, while Table 2 materials require placards when the aggregate gross weight reaches 1,001 lbs (454 kg) or more. Company rule: any hazmat load of 454 kg or more is treated as a placarded load, requires a hazmat-endorsed driver, and must be routed on approved hazmat corridors only.

## §4 Shipping Papers and Emergency Information
The driver must carry shipping papers describing each hazardous material, its UN number, hazard class, packing group and quantity. Shipping papers must be within immediate reach of the driver while at the wheel and must be left on the driver's seat or in the door pouch when the driver leaves the cab. Emergency response information (an ADR instructions-in-writing sheet or equivalent emergency response guide) and a 24-hour emergency contact number must accompany every hazmat load. In India the vehicle must display the emergency information panel prescribed under the CMVR dangerous goods rules.

## §5 Segregation and Co-Loading of Incompatible Classes
Incompatible hazard classes must never be loaded in the same vehicle. Explosives (Class 1) may not be co-loaded with any other hazard class. Oxidizers (Class 5.1) must not travel with flammable liquids (Class 3) or flammable solids (Class 4.1). Corrosives (Class 8) must be segregated from cyanides and toxic substances (Class 6.1). The route optimizer treats hazmat segregation as a hard constraint: a vehicle assignment that co-loads incompatible classes is rejected before a route is proposed.

## §6 Routing, Parking and Attendance
Hazmat vehicles must use designated hazmat routes and must avoid tunnels carrying an ADR tunnel restriction code that prohibits the load, as well as densely populated areas where an alternative route exists. A loaded hazmat vehicle must never be left unattended in a public place; the driver or a qualified person must remain within sight of the vehicle at all times except during a declared emergency. Hazmat vehicles may only be parked overnight in an approved secured depot or hazmat-designated parking area.

## §7 Mandatory Safety Equipment
Every hazmat vehicle must carry at least one 2 kg dry-powder fire extinguisher in the cab plus at least one 6 kg extinguisher suitable for the load, a spill containment kit, wheel chocks, two self-standing warning signs, eye-rinsing liquid and personal protective equipment (gloves, goggles, high-visibility vest) for each crew member. Missing or expired safety equipment makes the vehicle unfit for hazmat dispatch.
